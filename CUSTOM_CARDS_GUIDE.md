# Custom Racing Cards - Design Guide

## Overview

The **Custom Racing Cards** set introduces 18 new experimental abilities designed to expand the tactical depth of Drivers Momentum. This experimental set contains **82 cards** that can be integrated into the existing game or used as a standalone expansion.

## New Abilities Reference

### 🏎️ Offensive Abilities

#### SLIPSTREAM (x)
**Cost Value:** 4
**Description:** Follow directly behind a car within x segments, copying their next movement
**Strategic Use:**
- Powerful when trailing opponents
- Requires positioning skill
- Can leapfrog using opponent's strong cards
- Countered by staying in first place

**Balance Notes:**
- High ability value (4) reflects copying opponent movement
- Cost modifier -1 on some cards as it requires specific positioning
- Limited copies (3 cards) to prevent "follow the leader" gameplay

---

#### SABOTAGE
**Cost Value:** 2.5
**Description:** Target opponent's next card has -1 movement (minimum 0)
**Strategic Use:**
- Disrupts opponent planning
- Most effective against single-movement cards
- Can be used defensively to slow leaders
- Limited impact on multi-movement or WILD cards

**Balance Notes:**
- Moderate cost (2.5) as effect is temporary and limited
- 6 cards in set for availability without dominance

---

#### BUMPER
**Cost Value:** 2.5
**Description:** If you end movement adjacent to another car, push them 1 segment in any direction
**Strategic Use:**
- Tactical positioning tool
- Can push opponents into hazards
- Can help opponents escape trouble (political play)
- Requires ending movement near targets

**Balance Notes:**
- Conditional activation balances power
- 4 cards provide tactical options without overwhelming

---

### 🛡️ Defensive/Utility Abilities

#### REPAIR (x)
**Cost Value:** 3
**Description:** Remove up to x negative effects OR return x forgotten cards to deck
**Strategic Use:**
- Counter to oil slicks, obstacles, hooks
- Deck recovery mechanism
- Flexible choice between cleaning effects or deck rebuilding
- More valuable in longer games

**Balance Notes:**
- Moderate cost (3) for flexibility
- Limited copies (3 cards) maintain scarcity
- Dual-mode design rewards strategic thinking

---

#### SCOUT (x)
**Cost Value:** 2
**Description:** Look at the next x track segments and gain +1 XP
**Strategic Use:**
- Information advantage for planning
- Bonus XP makes it valuable even without tactical use
- Helps optimize card sequencing
- More powerful on unknown/modular tracks

**Balance Notes:**
- Low cost (2) as information doesn't directly affect board state
- +1 XP bonus justifies inclusion in decks
- 6 cards available without slowing gameplay

---

#### PRECISION
**Cost Value:** 2.5
**Description:** Ignore all obstacles and hazards on the track
**Strategic Use:**
- Hazard immunity for reliable movement
- Increases in value as hazards accumulate
- Enables aggressive pathing
- Counter to oil/obstacle strategies

**Balance Notes:**
- Moderate cost (2.5) for conditional immunity
- 4 cards provide options without nullifying hazard strategies

---

### ⚡ High-Risk Abilities

#### TURBO BOOST
**Cost Value:** 2
**Description:** Gain +2 movement this turn, then FORGET this card
**Strategic Use:**
- One-time power spike
- Strategic timing critical (key turns, final stretch)
- Deck thinning side effect (can be beneficial)
- Cannot be recovered with REPAIR

**Balance Notes:**
- Low cost (2) balanced by self-destruction
- Cost modifier -1 on most cards (cheap due to one-use)
- 5 cards allow strategic inclusion without deck clogging

---

#### OVERDRIVE
**Cost Value:** 3
**Description:** Double this card's movement, then take 1 unavoidable obstacle damage
**Strategic Use:**
- Massive movement spike with guaranteed cost
- Can be worth the damage on critical turns
- Punishes over-reliance (multiple uses = multiple damage)
- Pairs poorly with low-health situations

**Balance Notes:**
- Moderate cost (3) due to self-damage drawback
- Cost modifier -2 compensates for risk
- 3 cards limit damage accumulation

---

## Integration Guide

### Adding to Existing Game

The Custom Racing Cards can be integrated in several ways:

#### **Option 1: Full Integration**
Mix all 82 custom cards into the existing marketplace pools:
- Add to AutoShop (Basic) pool: ~40 cards
- Add to ChopShop (Advanced) pool: ~42 cards
- Increases card variety and strategic options

#### **Option 2: Expansion Set**
Create a third marketplace "Experimental Garage":
- Available after lap 2 or at XP threshold
- Premium/high-risk cards only
- Special purchase rules (e.g., requires FORGET 2 cards)

#### **Option 3: Modular Abilities**
Select specific ability groups based on desired gameplay:
- **Aggressive Pack**: Slipstream, Tailgate, Bumper, Sabotage
- **Technical Pack**: Drift, Precision, Scout, Momentum Shift
- **High-Risk Pack**: Turbo Boost, Overdrive, Shortcut
- **Strategic Pack**: Pit Stop, Mirror, Relay, Repair

#### **Option 4: Draft Format**
Use custom cards for draft-style deck building:
- Each player drafts 8-10 custom cards
- Combine with shared starter deck
- Creates unique deck identities per game

### Code Integration

To add these cards to the main card pool, update `/src/cards/index.ts`:

```typescript
import { CUSTOM_RACING_CARDS } from './cardSets/customCards'
```

## Balance Testing Checklist

When playtesting these cards, monitor for:

### Power Level Issues
- [ ] Turbo Boost: Is the self-forget penalty meaningful enough?
- [ ] Overdrive: Is 1 damage significant or trivial?
- [ ] Slipstream: Does it create "follow the leader" gameplay?
- [ ] Mirror: Can it create infinite loops with opponent abilities?
- [ ] Adrenaline: Does it cause too much rubber-banding?

### Gameplay Flow
- [ ] Scout: Does information analysis slow down turns?
- [ ] Pit Stop: Is deck manipulation too powerful in long games?
- [ ] Relay: Is sequencing too complex or just right?
- [ ] Momentum Shift: Does it eliminate meaningful momentum decisions?

### Cost Accuracy
- [ ] Are high-cost cards (8-10) worth purchasing?
- [ ] Are conditional cards (Tailgate, Adrenaline) priced fairly?
- [ ] Do one-use cards (Turbo Boost) feel like good value?

### Strategic Depth
- [ ] Do abilities create interesting decisions?
- [ ] Are there dominant strategies or "must-buy" cards?
- [ ] Do abilities interact well with existing mechanics?
- [ ] Is there healthy diversity in deck building?

## Design Philosophy

These custom cards follow several design principles:

### 1. **Conditional Power**
Many abilities require specific conditions (positioning, hand state, game state) to activate. This rewards skilled play without creating overpowered auto-includes.

### 2. **Risk/Reward Balance**
High-power abilities come with meaningful drawbacks:
- Turbo Boost: Powerful but self-destructs
- Overdrive: Double movement but guaranteed damage
- Slipstream: Strong but requires trailing position

### 3. **Thematic Resonance**
Every ability relates to real racing concepts:
- Slipstream = drafting
- Drift = controlled sliding
- Pit Stop = strategic servicing
- Shortcut = risk-taking alternate routes

### 4. **Strategic Depth**
Abilities encourage different playstyles:
- **Aggressive**: Bumper, Tailgate, Sabotage
- **Technical**: Precision, Scout, Drift
- **Economic**: Pit Stop, Repair, Adrenaline
- **Reactive**: Mirror, Backup Plan

### 5. **Interaction Encouragement**
Cards create player interaction beyond simple racing:
- Slipstream rewards trailing opponents
- Bumper enables pushing
- Mirror punishes power plays
- Exhaust creates zone control

- Persistent upgrades (carry between races)

### **Advanced Combos**
- Multi-card ability triggers
- Momentum-based scaling (more momentum = stronger effect)
